## Abstract

BEAD requires 99.45% uptime (≤48 hours downtime/year) but doesn't specify measurement methodology. This paper shows availability metrics vary along three independent dimensions — protocol layer (what's measured), measurement topology (where), and methodology (how, e.g. ITU-T Y.1540 vs. IETF RFC 9544) — and that two networks can both hit exactly 99.45% availability while having wildly different reliability, because availability is duration-weighted but frequency-blind.

## Key Contributions

- Surveys four current broadband availability measurement regimes (ISPs, application/content providers, independent commercial platforms, open research platforms) and shows they are not interchangeable.
- Proposes adopting ITU-T Y.1540's Percent IP Availability (PIA) framework over BEAD's existing test path, paired with an RFC 9544-style frequency/severity metric (Mean Time Between Violations) to close the frequency-blind gap.
- Argues broadband availability is now a regulatory metric, not just an engineering one, and that a standards-grounded definition is achievable without new numbers or protocols.
